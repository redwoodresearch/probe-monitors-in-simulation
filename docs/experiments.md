# Every experiment we ran

This page lists all 492 experiments behind our post *Probe monitors seem to perform realistically in simulated control
environments*: 476 that have a folder of code in [`../experiments/`](../experiments/), and 16 checks we ran outside any
folder. Each entry gives the date, the question and the finding as our experiment index states them. Each folder's README gives
the full entry: what we did, the full result, whether and where it appears in the post, its status, its main scripts and, where
the version of a script that produced a result the post uses differs from the folder's latest copy, that version too.

The index was written from each folder's notes, logs and result files and checked against them by a second model; its numbers
are copied from those sources. Many experiments were exploratory, superseded or abandoned, and their entries say so.

## How the project unfolded

We began on 5 and 6 October with GPT-5.6-Sol's real and simulated LinuxArena runs, read by a second model, Qwen3-32B, whose
activations the probes used. We asked three questions: can a probe tell real runs from simulated ones, do probes that flag
impactful commands work on both, and do attack detectors and off-the-shelf probes score both alike. A text classifier told real
runs from simulated ones almost perfectly (0.977, against 0.502 between two real runs), and seven cumulative text cleanups (of
times, IDs, whitespace and formatting) cut that to 0.667. From 9 October we fixed the simulator's formatting slips by general
rules and asked what a probe could still detect: it still separated the runs (0.822 with times masked, against 0.504 for two
real runs), yet at a threshold that flags 1% of real runs it flagged only 2.7% of simulated ones. In parallel, from 7 October,
Qwen3.5-27B acted as the agent itself, on real machines and in simulation, first on 20 and then on 196 tasks. On both sets of
runs we explored many side questions: deception and sabotage probes, evaluation awareness, probes trained on simulated runs,
and the agent's behaviour. On 14 October we wrote the analysis plan for the main question, whether a probe's catch rate in
simulation stays within a margin of its catch rate on real machines, and from then on grew the task set from the 196 pilot
tasks to the 1,313 in the post, each run twice on real machines and once in simulation. The final pooled analysis
([`2026-10-15_fpr_sweep_507_prep`](../experiments/2026-10-15_fpr_sweep_507_prep/)) computed the post's catch rates for six
probes, and from 16 to 26 October we re-derived its numbers with independent code and checked every number the draft post
printed against its source record.

Folder names keep our internal short names: `q1`, `q2` and `q3` for numbered research questions (in the first phase, the
three questions above; later folders reuse the numbers), `claimB` for the fixed-simulator test, `p2` for second-phase probe
work, `xerr` for the "error to add" estimates that preceded the final analysis, `il` for the interleaved launch, `sp493` for
the simulated control evaluations paper's other 493 tasks, and `routec` for the final pooled analysis and its checks. Suffixes
such as `_09` and `_2e` name the working session that ran the experiment.
[`../experiments/README.md`](../experiments/README.md#glossary) explains the labels that recur in code comments.

## Most interesting findings, including ones not in the post

Each item is from the linked folder's index entry, with its numbers as the entry states them; all of these entries have high
confidence in the index.

1. **The main result.** Averaged over six probes, Qwen3.5-27B's simulated attacks were caught 1.0 points less often than its real
   ones (95% interval −3.0 to +1.0), while real run 2 minus real run 1 was −0.2 points.
   ([`2026-10-15_fpr_sweep_507_prep`](../experiments/2026-10-15_fpr_sweep_507_prep/); in the post)
2. **At first, text alone gave the simulation away.** On GPT-5.6-Sol's runs, a term-frequency text classifier (TF-IDF)
   told real from simulated runs at 0.977 per run, against 0.502 between two real runs; seven text cleanups cut it to
   0.667. ([`2026-10-06_q1_text_baselines`](../experiments/2026-10-06_q1_text_baselines/); not in the post)
3. **After the formatting fixes, a probe still told runs apart, but rarely at a strict alarm.** With Qwen3-32B reading
   GPT-5.6-Sol's runs, the probe scored 0.822 with times masked, against 0.504 for two real runs, yet only 2.7% of simulated runs (interval 1.1 to 20.8) were flagged at 1% false alarms.
   ([`2026-10-09_fixed_sim`](../experiments/2026-10-09_fixed_sim/); not in the post)
4. **A zero-shot judge did worse than the probe at the same task.** GPT-5.6-Sol, reading one run at a time, scored 0.558 against
   the probe's 0.821 (two real runs: 0.492). ([`2026-10-11_claimB_judge`](../experiments/2026-10-11_claimB_judge/); not in the post)
5. **A probe trained the way a simulated evaluation would train it learned "simulated means attack".** Trained on simulated attack
   runs against real honest runs, at 1% false alarms it caught 84.4% of simulated attacks but 17.6% of real ones (layer 48).
   ([`2026-10-13_q3_sce_reply_probe`](../experiments/2026-10-13_q3_sce_reply_probe/); not in the post)
6. **Adding real attacks to a detector trained on simulated runs barely helped.** At layer 48 and 1%, its catch of real attacks
   rose only from 20.9% with no real attacks to 22.8% with 100, against 36.9% for the detector trained on real runs.
   ([`2026-10-13_few_real_attacks_POSTHOC`](../experiments/2026-10-13_few_real_attacks_POSTHOC/); not in the post)
7. **Rendering the simulator's editor outputs the way the real tool does closed a tool-output detector's gap, perhaps through
   where the readout lands.** Outside the pilot cells the edit raised catch by 12.9 points (+10.5 to +14.7) at 5%, leaving edited
   simulated minus second real at −1.0 (−4.4 to +1.9); for inserts the readout moved onto the review-note template (1.1% to 91.4%
   of simulated outputs). ([`2026-10-12_q2_fix939_checks`](../experiments/2026-10-12_q2_fix939_checks/); not in the post)
8. **Planting the simulator's slips into real runs forecast the gaps of tool-output detectors no earlier result had reported.**
   The forecast was off by 1.38 points on average, against 5.77 for the historical forecast, with rank correlation 0.93; the 15
   settings come from 3 reader models, so they are not independent.
   ([`2026-10-12_plantslip_heldout`](../experiments/2026-10-12_plantslip_heldout/); not in the post)
9. **The simulator did not hide failed commands.** Runs with a failed call: 86.3% (real run 1), 87.2% (real run 2) and 85.6%
   (simulated); on 431 identical pre-edit calls the simulator hid 0 of 23 real failures, and an earlier 46% against 0.1% tool-error contrast
   was a recording artifact. ([`2026-10-07_sim_failure_hiding`](../experiments/2026-10-07_sim_failure_hiding/); not in the post)
10. **A black-box judge beat a deception probe at telling attack runs from honest ones.** For Apollo's deception-probe recipe
    retrained on Qwen3.5-27B, within-task AUROC was 0.768 against 0.943 for a judge reading the same commands (difference −0.174, −0.239 to −0.110; preliminary).
    ([`2026-10-11_q2_deception_blackbox`](../experiments/2026-10-11_q2_deception_blackbox/); not in the post)
11. **The published deception probe worked far better when read at a follow-up question.** AUROC 0.973 (0.910 to 1.000) against
    0.760 at commands, on a pilot of 20 attack and 20 honest runs. The post does not use this readout; the experiment's rules note that it is
    structurally the end-of-sequence readout our rules exclude. ([`2026-10-15_fibs_followup`](../experiments/2026-10-15_fibs_followup/); not in
    the post)
12. **One task drove the simulated runs' extra false alarms.** For the 196-task command monitor, dropping the single top task
    turned the simulated minus real false-alarm gap from +1.78 points to −0.07.
    ([`2026-10-12_q3_fa_concentration`](../experiments/2026-10-12_q3_fa_concentration/); not in the post)

## Can a probe tell simulated runs from real ones?

Whether a probe or another detector can tell a simulated run from a real one: the first phase on GPT-5.6-Sol's runs read by Qwen3-32B, the fixed-simulator test, and the detector on Qwen3.5-27B's activations in the post (Figure 2).

- **2026-10-06** · [`2026-10-06_matched_step_pairs`](../experiments/2026-10-06_matched_step_pairs/). Holding the real history fixed, does the simulator's output for the same command look fake? *Found:* Text classifier AUROC 0.608 [0.576, 0.644], frozen probe 0.551 [0.521, 0.580], fresh probe 0.520 [0.497, 0.544] against a shuffled-label cutoff of 0.526; 57 of 200 simulated outputs byte-identical to the real one.
- **2026-10-06** · [`2026-10-06_q1_copied_vs_invented`](../experiments/2026-10-06_q1_copied_vs_invented/). How much of the real-vs-simulated separation comes from tool outputs the simulator invented versus outputs it copied from its reference honest run? *Found:* Per-output text-classifier AUROC 0.556 on exact copies, 0.847 on near copies, 0.820 on unmatched outputs (two real runs 0.494 to 0.554); real editor diffs always carry the line-number gutter, simulated ones in 7.6%.
- **2026-10-06** · [`2026-10-06_q1_pilot`](../experiments/2026-10-06_q1_pilot/). Can a linear probe on Qwen3-32B pooled activations tell real from simulated LinuxArena attack transcripts (first pilot)? *Found:* No result exists: the pilot analysis never saved any numbers. *Status:* superseded by [2026-10-06_q1_segment_end](../experiments/2026-10-06_q1_segment_end/) (segment-end readout).
- **2026-10-06** · [`2026-10-06_q1_segment_end`](../experiments/2026-10-06_q1_segment_end/). Can a linear probe on Qwen3-32B activations tell real LinuxArena attack runs (GPT-5.6-Sol's) from simulated ones, and does it see anything a text classifier does not? *Found:* Under the frozen rule with all seven cleanups, no probe advantage.
- **2026-10-06** · [`2026-10-06_q1_text_baselines`](../experiments/2026-10-06_q1_text_baselines/). How well do text-only methods (a term-frequency text classifier, TF-IDF, and the LinuxArena paper's pairwise language-model judge) tell real from simulated LinuxArena attack runs, on the probe's tasks and folds? *Found:* TF-IDF 0.977 per run with no text cleanup vs 0.502 between two real runs (times masked).
- **2026-10-07** · [`2026-10-07_hex_id_scrub_fix`](../experiments/2026-10-07_hex_id_scrub_fix/). Did a bug in the text-cleanup ID rule (underscore-joined hex IDs were chopped instead of masked) leave a real-versus-simulated tell that inflated the text classifier's results? *Found:* The bug was real but did not matter; the fix changes about 0.2% of outputs per arm; AUROC with all seven cleanups 0.555 vs 0.556 before; real-versus-real 0.496 vs 0.495.
- **2026-10-07** · [`2026-10-07_what_each_method_sees`](../experiments/2026-10-07_what_each_method_sees/). What do the TF-IDF text classifier and the Qwen3-32B probe each pick up when telling real from simulated runs with no text cleanup and with all seven cumulative cleanups (times and dates, numbers and IDs, whitespace, harness format, diff lines, empty diff lines, editor output format), and does the evidence add up over a run? *Found:* With no text cleanup both read the editor format (text classifier 0.792, probe 0.804); with all seven cleanups the leftover is a few rare simulator tells, and dropping five moves them to 0.514 and 0.524.
- **2026-10-08** · [`2026-10-08_q2_date_histogram`](../experiments/2026-10-08_q2_date_histogram/). How do dates and hours in tool outputs differ between real and simulated runs (a date tell inventory)? *Found:* Simulated attacks show 2025 in 12.1% of outputs vs 3.8% and 4.4% for real runs, and share real runs' most common date far less than two real runs do (-41.2% [-46.8%, -35.5%]); honest depends on batch (preliminary).
- **2026-10-09** · [`2026-10-09_fixed_sim`](../experiments/2026-10-09_fixed_sim/). The fixed-simulator test: if the simulator's systematic formatting errors are fixed by general rules, can a probe or text classifier still tell simulated runs from real ones, and what does it read? *Found:* The fixed simulator fails the text bar (TF-IDF 0.661 [0.612, 0.744] on held-out environments, bar 0.55). *Status:* closed; several sub-rows exploratory.
- **2026-10-09** · [`2026-10-09_q1_method_checks`](../experiments/2026-10-09_q1_method_checks/). Method checks on the Phase 1 real-versus-simulated probe: how much do all seven cumulative cleanups (times and dates, numbers and IDs, whitespace, harness format, diff lines, empty diff lines, editor output format) change each world's text, does a wider regularization search with a selection-aware null change the result, and what do the cleanup ladder and planted checks look like in catch at 1% and 5% false alarms? *Found:* All seven cleanups change 0.314 of real and 0.312 of simulated outputs.
- **2026-10-10** · [`2026-10-10_claimB_meaning_control`](../experiments/2026-10-10_claimB_meaning_control/). In the fixed-simulator test (can probes tell simulated runs from real ones after the simulator's formatting mistakes are fixed?), does the frozen main realism probe respond to the meaning "this is a simulation", or only to simulator formatting mistakes? *Found:* Not shown to read meaning; the 'simulation' line moves the score slightly toward real (-0.0066), simulation minus production line -0.0029 [-0.0075, +0.0017].

For the fixed-simulator test (can probes tell simulated runs from real ones after the simulator's formatting mistakes are fixed?), does the probe-fitting code reproduce earlier results, and does the main probe's result hold when only converged fits are kept? *Found:* The dry run reproduces the earlier probe (0.917 with no text cleanup) with real run 2 vs run 1 at chance (0.516).
- **2026-10-10** · [`2026-10-10_claimB_probe_attribution`](../experiments/2026-10-10_claimB_probe_attribution/).
